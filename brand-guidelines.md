# SuperGP - Brand Guidelines

> Version 2.0 | August 2026 | Source of truth for the SuperGP marketing site (practice-clarity-pro)

---

### Scope of this version

This update resolves a fragmentation problem: four different colour/type systems were live at once across the logo brief, the product app, its design-system.html, and this marketing site — none of which fully delivered on "disruptive, tech-first, simple." This version unifies **visual identity, logo, colour, and typography** for the marketing site around one electric, dark-first system. Brand foundation, positioning, messaging, and voice below were already consistent and are carried forward unchanged.

The product app's own in-product UI theme is intentionally **out of scope** here and may continue to run its current light "calm clinical" system separately — that's a decision for a future pass.

## 1. Brand foundation

### Purpose
SuperGP exists to remove operational chaos in GP practices so teams can spend more time on patient care and less time on admin.

### Vision
A healthcare system where every GP practice runs on one modern operating system: compliance-ready, operationally clear, and clinically focused.

### Mission
Replace spreadsheets, fragmented tools, and manual workarounds with one trusted platform designed by NHS clinicians and built by experienced technology leaders.

### Values
1. **Clinical empathy**
   We build from frontline experience, not assumptions.
2. **Operational clarity**
   We make complex daily operations visible and manageable.
3. **Dependable execution**
   Practice teams can rely on SuperGP every day, across every site.
4. **Straight-talking expertise**
   We communicate clearly, back claims with evidence, and avoid hype.

### Brand personality
- **Direct**: clear statements, no corporate filler.
- **Credible**: specific, evidence-led, practical.
- **Empathetic**: understands stress and workload pressure.
- **Disruptive**: a deliberate visual and tonal break from NHS-blue, legacy-software, corporate healthcare IT conventions.
- **Reliable**: calm, trustworthy, and consistently useful.

### Brand promise
Less admin. More patient care.

## 2. Positioning

### Target audiences
- **Primary**: Practice Managers and Operations Leads in UK GP practices.
- **Secondary**: GP Partners, Clinical Directors, and PCN leaders.
- **Tertiary**: Multi-site decision makers evaluating operational software.

### Market problem
Most practices still run core operations through spreadsheets, messages, and disconnected systems. This creates rota instability, compliance risk, unused capacity, and avoidable admin load.

### Differentiation
SuperGP is built by working NHS GPs and experienced product and engineering leaders. It combines clinical understanding with modern software execution in one platform — and it looks like a modern tech product, not a corporate healthcare IT relic.

### Positioning statement
For UK GP practices under operational pressure, SuperGP is the modern practice OS that replaces fragmented tools with one connected system, giving teams visibility, control, and confidence.

### Brand pillars
1. **Built for real practice operations**
2. **One system across people, sites, and compliance**
3. **Audit-ready by default**
4. **Scales from one site to many**

## 3. Messaging architecture

### Core lines
- **Primary headline**: Run your practice, without the chaos.
- **Sub-headline**: Less admin. More patient care.
- **Founder credibility line**: Designed by GPs. Engineered by experts.

### Value proposition
SuperGP replaces spreadsheets and disconnected admin tools with one platform for rota planning, capacity visibility, task execution, and audit-ready compliance.

### Proof points and approved usage
Use quantitative claims when context allows and evidence is available:
- **Admin reduction**: "Cut admin by up to 70%."
- **Operational waste**: "15-20% of appointment slots often go unused without clear staffing visibility."
- **Cost pressure**: "Spreadsheet-led rota management can contribute to significant locum overspend."
- **Audit confidence**: "Move from reactive evidence gathering to always-on audit readiness."

### Audience message blocks
- **Practice managers**: "Stop firefighting rota gaps and task bottlenecks. SuperGP gives you one command centre for daily operations."
- **GP partners**: "Reduce admin load so clinicians and support teams can focus on patient demand."
- **PCN and multi-site leads**: "Plan, coordinate, and monitor staffing and capacity across every site in one system."

### Feature message hierarchy
Present features in this order for most sales and marketing surfaces:
1. **Smart Rota Planner** - scheduling stability, conflict prevention, faster publishing.
2. **Dashboard** - real-time visibility of staffing, rooms, tasks, and capacity.
3. **Task Manager** - repeatable workflows and traceable execution.
4. **Sites Manager** - multi-site control, scaling, and facility planning.
5. **Teams Manager** - unified workforce data, role and permission control.
6. **Policy GPT** - private, grounded SOP retrieval with ring-fenced data.

## 4. Voice and tone

### Voice characteristics
- **Direct**: lead with the problem or outcome in the first line.
- **Empathetic**: acknowledge pressure points before presenting solutions.
- **Confident**: specific claims, practical wording, no exaggeration.
- **Action-oriented**: use active verbs and clear next steps.

### Tone by context
- **Marketing headlines**: bold, punchy, challenge-led.
- **Feature pages**: clear, benefit-first, operationally specific.
- **Product UX copy**: calm, concise, instructional.
- **Support and onboarding**: reassuring, practical, never blaming.
- **Compliance content**: precise, plain English, fully auditable.

### Writing standards
- Use UK English (`practice`, `organise`, `licence` where applicable).
- Prefer active voice and short-to-medium sentences.
- Use numerals for stats and impact claims.
- Spell out acronyms on first use (for example, Primary Care Network (PCN)).

### Approved and avoided language
- **Use more of**: `in one place`, `audit-ready`, `in seconds`, `real-time visibility`, `reduce admin`.
- **Use less of**: `revolutionary`, `seamless`, `game-changing`, `best-in-class`, `leverage`.
- **Never use**: vague hype with no practical outcome.

### Do and do not examples
- **Do**: "Your rota should not collapse every week. SuperGP flags conflicts before you publish."
- **Do not**: "Our innovative platform leverages modern technology for workforce optimisation."
- **Do**: "Manage all sites from one dashboard."
- **Do not**: "Facilitate cross-location operational synergies."

## 6. Visual identity direction

### Design principle
**Disruptive. Tech-first. Simple.** Every visual decision should read as a clear break from NHS-blue, spreadsheet-era healthcare software, and generic corporate SaaS templates — while staying legible, credible, and calm enough for a clinical audience. Dark, electric, and minimal beats busy, bright, and "friendly-clip-art" healthcare marketing.

### Logo system
- **Concept**: Hexagon badge with an implicit "S" curve, formed through negative space (per the original creative brief). The hexagon signals structure and system design; the "S" ties directly to "Super." This concept is retained.
- **Colour — updated**: the mark moves from Teal + Coral to the brand's electric palette: **Electric Indigo (`#5B4FFF`)** hexagon/primary stroke, with **Electric Cyan (`#00D9F7`)** available as a two-tone accent on the S-curve for marketing contexts. This replaces teal/coral entirely — teal reads as generic healthcare; electric indigo/cyan reads as modern tech.
- **Monochrome**: white mark on dark surfaces (primary usage) or black mark on white (print/limited-colour contexts) always available as a fallback.
- Required lockups: icon-only mark, horizontal lockup (icon + wordmark), stacked/vertical lockup.
- Clear space: minimum equals the height of the hexagon on all sides.
- Minimum digital size: 32px (icon), 120px width for the full horizontal wordmark lockup.
- Do not stretch, distort, add drop shadows/3D effects, recolour outside the approved palette, or place on busy backgrounds without contrast support.
- File formats: SVG (primary, web), PDF/AI (print/source), PNG (transparent, raster fallback). Optimise SVGs (target <5KB).

### Colour palette

SuperGP's marketing identity is **dark-first and electric** — the visual signal that this is a modern technology company, not a healthcare IT incumbent.

**Brand colours:**
| Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Electric Indigo | `#5B4FFF` | Primary — logo, CTAs, interactive elements, focus rings |
| Electric Cyan | `#00D9F7` | Accent — gradient tail, info states, links, logo two-tone |
| Electric Lime | `#A8FF3E` | Success — positive metrics, proof points ("70% saved") |
| Soft Violet | `#8B5CF6` | AI features — Policy GPT, depth accents |

**Signature gradient:**
`linear-gradient(135deg, #5B4FFF 0%, #00D9F7 100%)`
Used on: hero headline text spans, primary CTAs, key accent elements, logo two-tone variant.

**Semantic colours:**
| Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Warm Amber | `#FFB830` | Warnings, pending states |
| Hot Red | `#FF4D6A` | Errors, critical alerts |

**Dark surfaces (default, marketing):**
| Name | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| BG | `#070B14` | Deepest background |
| Surface 1 | `#0D1422` | Card / panel backgrounds |
| Surface 2 | `#121929` | Elevated panels |
| Surface 3 | `#1A2236` | Hover states |

**Text on dark:**
- Primary text: `#F0F4FF`
- Secondary text: `rgba(240,244,255,0.55)`
- Muted/labels: `rgba(240,244,255,0.30)`

**Retired from marketing use**: Teal (`#0F766E`), Coral, warm sand/charcoal neutrals. These conflicted with the electric direction and read closer to a generic wellness/healthcare template than a tech disruptor.

**Colour rules:**
- Never use NHS blue (`#005EB8`) or generic corporate blues as primary brand colours.
- Never use the retired teal/coral/warm-sand palette on new marketing work.
- Electric Lime replaces conventional green for success states — it is intentionally bold.
- Glow effects (`box-shadow` with brand colour at low opacity) are on-brand for primary interactive elements and hero moments — this is one of the ways the marketing site keeps its own expressive, storytelling energy distinct from the more restrained in-product UI.

### Typography

**One typeface family, everywhere.** This is a deliberate simplicity choice — no separate display/editorial fonts (Space Grotesk, Chakra Petch, Satoshi are retired from the marketing site).

- **Primary typeface**: Inter (400, 500, 600, 700, and 800/900 for large marketing display type).
- **Monospace support typeface**: JetBrains Mono — used sparingly for eyebrows, technical labels, and stat/token-style callouts, reinforcing the "engineered" feel.
- Use sentence case for headlines and labels.
- Large display/hero type may use tighter tracking (−0.03em to −0.04em) and heavier weights (800–900) to carry the "bold, tech-forward" tone without needing a second typeface.
- Keep body copy readable and concise; target 60–72 characters per line in long-form contexts.

### Marketing site's distinct identity

The marketing site is not a literal copy of the in-product UI — it's allowed (expected) to feel more expressive: bigger type, glow/gradient accents, motion (shimmer CTAs, floating elements, subtle parallax), and longer-form storytelling sections. What keeps it "on brand" rather than "a different brand" is that all of this is built from the **same colour tokens and the same typeface** documented above, rather than a separate palette or font stack. Distinct feel, shared system.

### Imagery and iconography
- Use authentic clinical and operational contexts over generic stock imagery.
- Prefer product-led visuals where possible (UI, workflow states, dashboards) rendered on dark surfaces with electric accent glows.
- Keep icon style consistent (outline style, uniform stroke weight).

### Accessibility standard
All production usage must meet WCAG AA contrast requirements (minimum 4.5:1 for normal text). Verify electric accent colours against dark surfaces specifically — Electric Cyan and Electric Lime pass comfortably on `#070B14`/`#0D1422`; always check any new colour pairing before shipping.

## 7. Brand protection and governance

### Legal and naming controls
- Protect `SuperGP` as a word mark in relevant UK classes and territories.
- Use approved product naming consistently: `SuperGP`, `Smart Rota Planner`, `Policy GPT`.
- Do not permit third-party modification of logos, names, or taglines.

### Partner and press usage
- Maintain a controlled press kit with approved logos, screenshots, and boilerplate copy.
- Require review before partner pages, marketplace listings, and event collateral go live.
- Use "Works with SuperGP" for integrations rather than substituting the core brand mark.

### Brand compliance checklist
Before publishing any customer-facing asset, confirm:
- [ ] Correct logo lockup, colour (Electric Indigo/Cyan, not teal/coral), and safe area used.
- [ ] Approved colour and type system used (electric palette; Inter + JetBrains Mono only).
- [ ] UK English and SuperGP voice standards applied.
- [ ] Messaging aligns to approved positioning and proof points.
- [ ] Accessibility checks completed.
- [ ] Legal and privacy claims reviewed where relevant.

### Governance cadence
- **Monthly**: copy and campaign spot checks across web, product, and sales collateral.
- **Quarterly**: full brand consistency review and message performance assessment.
- **Biannually**: strategic refresh of messaging priorities and proof points.
- **Owner**: Product/Brand lead with sign-off from Clinical and Technology leadership.

### Escalation rule
Any new tagline, major claim, or visual identity change must be reviewed before public release.

## 8. Quick implementation guide

### Priority rollout sequence
1. Update the logo mark to Electric Indigo/Cyan across the marketing site (favicon, header, footer, social).
2. Migrate marketing site typography from Space Grotesk/Chakra Petch/Satoshi to Inter + JetBrains Mono.
3. Replace teal/coral/warm-sand tokens in `src/index.css` and `tailwind.config.ts` with the electric palette and dark-surface tokens documented above.
4. Re-test hero, CTA, card, and glow/shimmer components against the new tokens for contrast and visual consistency.
5. Run first quarterly brand audit using the compliance checklist.
6. Revisit the in-product app theme as a separate, future decision — it is intentionally out of scope of this version.

### Definition of done
Marketing site brand implementation is complete when the logo, colour tokens, and typography above are live sitewide, with no remaining teal/coral/warm-sand or Space Grotesk/Chakra Petch/Satoshi references.
